Your secret information (for adventurers)

Application: Donjons & Savons
Region: Switzerland
Audience: Players under 18
Version: 1, 22 September 2026

This page explains what information the game keeps about you, and how it is protected. If you have any questions, ask an adult in your clan. The rules of the game are on another page.

1. What the game knows about you

Almost nothing! The game only keeps:

  • your nickname, your avatar and what you tell about your character,
  • your experience, your level, your hit points and your items,
  • the fact that you are a child (but not your date of birth: the game forgets it straight away),
  • your clan's adventures (the chores defeated, the clan journal).

2. What the game does NOT know

  • The photos and videos you take stay on the phone. They are never sent to the Internet, and the game shows them to nobody. Your videos last 15 seconds at most and never record sound. You alone can look at them again, and it is you who decides to show them, for instance to the adult in your clan who checks your work. And as soon as your chore has been checked, the photo or video erases itself: it is no longer any use.
  • The game knows neither your real name, nor your address, nor your school. (So do not write them in your nickname!)
  • If you log in with your own Google account, your email address stays with Google: the game does not keep it with your player information. A number is all it needs to recognise you.
  • There is no advertising, and nobody spies on you to show you any.

3. Who can see your nickname?

Only the members of your clan. Outside your clan, the game gives you another name, a made-up one that looks nothing like yours. If you play in several clans, the members of your other clans also see your nickname, your avatar and your title, and nothing else. No other clan can see who you are or what you do, and there is no list of players anywhere. There is no chat: you cannot talk to anyone you do not know in the game.

4. Your clan's story

When the clan's chest is opened, the game can write a lovely story telling your adventures. To write it, it sends your clan's journal (with the players' nicknames and the tasks you have defeated) to a computer that writes the text. Then an adult in your clan decides whether to share that story, or keep it for yourselves.

5. Your parents watch over you

It is an adult responsible for you who gave permission for the game to keep your information, by accepting the rules and bringing you into their clan. They can look at it and make it disappear whenever they want. But first, you were the one who said you wanted to play: they saw your answer before deciding, and it is kept together with their decision.

If you were already playing on the phone of one of your parents, they can give you back your character when you get your own phone: you get back your level, your items and your clan's adventures.

When they brought you in, the game reminded them that they are declaring they are your legal guardian. If you were already in another clan, one of the grown-ups who answer for you had said yes beforehand, and the game checked it. For the rest, they are the one who answers for what they declared: the game cannot tell on its own who your parents are. Their own rules are in the grown-ups' privacy policy.

You can play in several clans, for example in your grandparents' clan, if your parents agree. They can also take you out of a clan. Your nickname, your avatar and your title are the same in all your clans. Your level, your items and your adventures stay in each clan. And you can turn off the notifications of one clan, or of all of them.

6. Where your information is kept

Right at the start, the game asks you to choose a region of the world: that is where your information is kept, and it never moves from there. For now it is Switzerland, but your information itself is kept on computers in Europe. Each region has its own rules, because the laws are not the same everywhere.

Two small things still go through Google's computers elsewhere in the world, because the game needs them to work: the number that lets the game recognise you when you log in, and the notifications that arrive on your phone. They stay with Google, which looks after the game's computers for us, and nobody else sees them. Everything else (your character, your clan, your adventures) stays in your region.

7. Making your information disappear

If you or your parents no longer want to play, your account can be deleted. An adult in your clan can do it, and so can you, on your own, from the game: you need nobody's permission. It is also possible on the page donjons.grisloup.com/delete-account.

Here is what happens, in order:

  • Straight away: you disappear from the game. Your character, your nickname and your adventures are no longer visible anywhere, to anyone.
  • For 30 days: your information is put away in a closed chest, outside the game. Nobody uses it. It is just in case someone pressed the button by mistake.
  • After that: it is erased for good, like magic. We only keep a note of the day you said you wanted to play, of the day your parents said yes, and of the day they said stop, so we can prove it if we are ever asked. That note is erased too, after 5 years.

And what if nobody pays for your clan's subscription any more? The clan goes on pause: nobody can play in it, but nothing is erased straight away. Your character, your level, the chores defeated and the clan journal are kept for two years. Why so long? Because a family that stops has not always said goodbye to the game: people move house, children grow up, a new school year begins. If the clan takes up the subscription again during those two years, you find everything just as it was. If not, the grown-ups are warned a month before, and then the clan is erased in the same way.

And what if nobody in your clan opens the game for a very, very long time? After two years without a single visit, and if nobody is paying for the subscription, the clan is erased in the same way. But the grown-ups are warned several times first, and all it takes is for someone in the clan, you included, to open the game again for nothing to happen.

And what if you signed in but never got into a clan? After 30 days without opening the game, your account is erased.

Careful: this is final. If you play again later, you will start over from scratch, with a new character.

8. The beta list

On the game's website, grown-ups can leave their email address to be told when a new version is ready to try out. That is not in the game, and it is not for you: you are never asked for an address. This list holds no information about children, and it will be wiped completely once the tests are over.

9. Any questions?

An adult in your clan can write to donjons@grisloup.com.
